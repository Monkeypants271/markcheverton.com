# StoryDogs: exact added ideas

Added 12 examples to each of 14 banks, increasing every bank from 12 to 24 examples. All existing examples are preserved.

## HERO — Who’s Your Hero?

### Field 1: Who is your main character? Give them a name and tell us a little about them.

- Milo is a vampire who faints at the sight of blood.
- Dot is a polite goblin who runs a tiny bank.
- Bram is a minotaur who bakes perfect cinnamon rolls.
- Ada is a kid detective with a magnifying glass.
- Ben is a new kid who loves making paper airplanes.
- Zoe is a soccer player who cheers for both teams.
- Pickles is a pirate mouse searching for lost cheese.
- Fern is a witch whose broom only sweeps floors.
- Gus is a ghost who leaves friendly notes.
- Ruby is a kid who rescues worms after rain.
- Dash is a snail who wants to explore a mountain.
- Poppy is a talking pumpkin who loves bedtime stories.

### Field 2: What is something they struggle with or are afraid of?

- They faint when they see even a tiny drop of blood.
- They are good with numbers but afraid to speak up.
- Their horns keep poking holes in their baking hat.
- They jump whenever their own shadow moves.
- They rush and forget to check their clues.
- They feel shy asking to join a game.
- They get upset when their team loses.
- They are afraid to try riding without training wheels.
- They know magic but cannot tie their shoes.
- They worry nobody will like their homemade gift.
- They have trouble waiting for their turn.
- They want to explore but dislike getting muddy.

## WISH — A Wish and an Ordinary Day

### Field 1: What is your character’s life like before the adventure begins?

- They count coins at a bank run by polite goblins.
- They bake bread in a kitchen built for minotaurs.
- They sleep all day and grow tomatoes at night.
- They walk their little brother to school each morning.
- They help feed animals at the town shelter.
- They trade homemade comics with friends at recess.
- They sail a leaf boat across the duck pond.
- They repair umbrellas for mermaids who dislike getting wet.
- They deliver soup to dragons with sore throats.
- They dust a quiet attic with a friendly ghost.
- They check muddy footprints outside their garden gate.
- They live above a shop that sells invisible hats.

### Field 2: What does your character want, and why does it matter to them?

- They want to bake a cake that fits every friend.
- They want to open a bank for lost tooth-fairy coins.
- They want a night picnic so their vampire friend can come.
- They want to learn to swim before the class trip.
- They want to fix a bike for their little sister.
- They want to earn seeds for a butterfly garden.
- They want to find who keeps leaving kind notes.
- They want to return a ghost's missing blanket.
- They want to find an island shaped like a waffle.
- They want to teach a shy dragon to dance.
- They want to make the new student feel welcome.
- They want to find their dog's favorite squeaky toy.

## UH-OH! — Uh-Oh… Everything Changes!

### Field 1: What happens that changes your character’s normal life?

- The birthday cake floats out the kitchen window.
- A goblin banker finds a dragon egg in the safe.
- The minotaur's bread starts singing instead of rising.
- Someone leaves muddy paw prints on the classroom floor.
- Their dog comes home wearing a tiny crown.
- A friendly ghost knocks and asks to borrow socks.
- Their shadow waves before they do.
- The playground slide starts sending kids to a candy castle.
- They find a map tucked inside a library book.
- Their team's ball rolls into a locked shed.
- The family picnic basket is missing.
- A shy monster moves into the treehouse next door.

### Field 2: What does your character decide to do because of it?

- They follow the cake in a hot-air balloon.
- They ask the goblin banker to help find the egg's family.
- They try singing along with the bread.
- They compare the paw prints with their pet's paws.
- They make a welcome card for the shy monster.
- They lend the ghost their warmest pair of socks.
- They ask their shadow what it wants to say.
- They ask a grown-up to help open the shed.
- They search for the picnic basket with their family.
- They invite a friend to study the hidden map.
- They offer to help the new student find their classroom.
- They leave a kind note for the mystery visitor.

## TROUBLE — Here Comes Trouble!

### Field 1: What problem gets in the way when your character tries to reach their goal?

- The floating cake gets stuck in a tall tree.
- The dragon egg hatches during the bank's quiet hour.
- The minotaur cannot fit through the bakery door.
- The clue smells like cookies, but every shop sells cookies.
- The ghost's sneeze blows out all the lanterns.
- A troll wants a bedtime story before opening the gate.
- Their shoelace breaks just before the race.
- Their friend thinks they hid the missing toy.
- The treasure chest has a lock shaped like a banana.
- The friendly monster is too shy to answer questions.
- Their paper boat springs a leak.
- The dog keeps burying every clue they find.

### Field 2: What do they try, and how does that create the next problem?

- They shake the tree, and the cake lands on a branch higher up.
- They feed the baby dragon, and it follows them everywhere.
- They shrink the minotaur's hat, but his horns stay big.
- They follow the cookie smell into the wrong shop.
- They relight a lantern, and the ghost sneezes again.
- They read to the troll, who asks for ten more stories.
- They tie their shoes together and cannot run.
- They search alone, and their friend feels left out.
- They offer the lock a banana, attracting hungry monkeys.
- They shout their question, making the monster hide.
- They patch the boat, but leave their paddle on shore.
- They dig up one clue and cover another with dirt.

## YIKES! — Trouble Gets an Upgrade!

### Field 1: How does the situation become even more difficult?

- The cake starts melting in the afternoon sun.
- The baby dragon hiccups sparks near the bank's papers.
- The singing bread wakes every baby in town.
- The next clue is hidden by falling leaves.
- The ghost loses the lantern just as night falls.
- The troll falls asleep across the only path.
- Their bike gets a flat tire far from home.
- Their friend packs up and leaves the game.
- The monkeys carry the treasure chest into the trees.
- The shy monster thinks the welcome party is a trap.
- Water rises over the paper boat's sides.
- The dog buries the map just before a rainstorm.

### Field 2: What could your character lose if they fail now?

- Their friend might have no cake for the birthday picnic.
- The goblins could lose the coins saved for a playground.
- The bakery might miss its biggest order.
- They might never find who sent the kind notes.
- The ghost could miss the midnight lantern parade.
- Their neighbors might miss the path to the picnic.
- They might miss their sister's first school play.
- Their friend might stop inviting them to play.
- The treasure's owner might never get their keepsake back.
- The monster might move away without making a friend.
- Their boat's tiny passenger could lose its ride home.
- They might miss the surprise waiting at the map's end.

## SHOWDOWN — Time for the Big Moment!

### Field 1: What is the biggest challenge your character must face?

- They must catch the cake before it melts.
- They must calm the baby dragon without waking its family.
- They must bake the whole order with a broken oven.
- They must put the muddy clues in the right order.
- They must guide the ghost through the dark attic.
- They must wake the troll without making it grumpy.
- They must ask for help fixing their flat tire.
- They must say sorry before their friend leaves.
- They must trade the monkeys something for the treasure.
- They must help the monster feel safe at the party.
- They must get the tiny passenger safely to shore.
- They must find the buried map before the rain starts.

### Field 2: What do they choose to do, and what happens because of that choice?

- They spread a picnic blanket, and the cake lands safely.
- They hum softly, and the baby dragon falls asleep.
- They ask a neighbor to share an oven and finish baking.
- They compare the clues and find the missing puppy.
- They hold the ghost's hand and find the lantern together.
- They whisper a funny rhyme, and the troll wakes smiling.
- They ask a trusted grown-up, who helps repair the tire.
- They say sorry and invite their friend to choose the game.
- They trade bananas for the chest and return the treasure.
- They offer the monster a quiet seat, and it stays.
- They use their hat as a scoop and reach shore.
- They follow the dog's muddy paws and uncover the map.

## AFTER! — What’s Different Now?

### Field 1: What has your character learned, or how have they changed?

- They learn that a quiet voice can solve a loud problem.
- They learn to ask before borrowing a friend's things.
- They discover that a scary-looking neighbor can be kind.
- They learn to check clues before guessing.
- They learn that saying sorry is only the first step.
- They learn to include friends with different needs.
- They discover that slow work can be careful work.
- They learn that brave people can still feel afraid.
- They learn to share the job and the credit.
- They learn that even goblins can have good manners.
- They discover that their horns make handy dough hooks.
- They learn that a small kindness can start a friendship.

### Field 2: What is their life like at the end of the story?

- They share cake with the whole neighborhood.
- The baby dragon naps beside the goblin bank's welcome mat.
- The minotaur opens a bakery with extra-wide doors.
- They start a detective club with their friends.
- The ghost joins them for flashlight story nights.
- They bring the troll a new library book each week.
- They ride bikes with their sister after school.
- They take turns choosing games with their friend.
- They keep a banana key beside the treasure chest.
- The monster waves from its treehouse every morning.
- They build a stronger boat for their next pond trip.
- Their vampire friend opens a midnight tomato-juice cafe.
