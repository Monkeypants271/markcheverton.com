If your kid loves Minecraft, you already know the kind of stories they're drawn to: survival against the odds, monsters in the dark, building something out of nothing, and a hero who has to outsmart a world that's trying to take them down. So when they ask for "a book like Minecraft," what they really want is a book that gives them those same feelings.

Here's how to find one, and where to start.

## What makes a book feel "like Minecraft"

Before any specific title, it helps to know what you're actually looking for. The books that land hardest with Minecraft fans almost always share a few ingredients:

- **Survival stakes.** A character who has to stay alive in a dangerous world — limited resources, real threats, no easy way out.
- **Monsters and danger.** Zombies, skeletons, spiders, and worse, lurking just past the torchlight.
- **Teamwork.** A small crew of friends who have to rely on each other to make it through.
- **Building and problem-solving.** Heroes who win with cleverness — crafting, planning, and figuring things out — not just brute strength.
- **An open, game-like world.** A setting big enough to explore, with the sense that anything could be over the next hill.
- **Fast pacing.** Short chapters and cliffhangers that make "just one more chapter" impossible to resist.

Match those, and you've matched what your kid loves about the game.

## How to actually get them reading it

- **Lean into the obsession.** If they love Minecraft, hand them the Minecraft-inspired books first. Don't make them "earn" their way to fun reading.
- **Start at Book One and let momentum carry them.** Series are perfect for reluctant readers because finishing one book creates a built-in reason to grab the next.
- **Keep it pressure-free.** No quizzes, no logs — just let it feel like an extension of the game they already love.

The goal isn't one book. It's that moment when a kid who "doesn't read" suddenly can't wait to find out what happens next.
